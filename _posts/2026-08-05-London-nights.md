When I arrived at Nisha's house party, people were crowded around a giant, purple sweet potato. A guest had brought it as a gift, and people were taking turns holding it to their heads to see which was bigger. Old Nirvana blasted in the background, and Nisha pressed a lukewarm vodka cranberry into my hand. She spoke into my ear as I eyed the kilo of ket resting on the coffee table, untroubled. <br>
"We won't be here long, just grabbing a few things, and the we head to the club."

I nodded and turned my attention to a separate cluster on the sofa. A man with a French accent, and an oversized blue crew neck was complaining about how his job as a financial consultant was draining his soul. Everyone nodded around him sympathetically, as if comrades. By the time they were admitting feeling directionless and stuck in their 20s, my interest had jaded. 

A group of us stepped out into the night. Me, Nisha and Yeonseo were mutual friends from Uni. The boys were Mateo, and Lucas - Nisha's mates from interrailing, and Olly was her work colleague. We raised a ruckus on the tube, arguing different moral and philosophical topics. Such as the trolley problem: a train is heading toward five workers, you can pull a lever to divert it onto another track where it will kill just one. Or you can do nothing. Most chose to kill the one person to save five. Me and Mateo were not so sure. I reckon I would also pull the lever, but I would never recover from the deed. So, really, I just killed myself too. Or the version of myself before the action. 

We finally got to the club, the Victoria, in Dalston. It was lively, with the smell of ale, social anxiety, and impending infidelity. While we were waiting to order a drink, Yeonseo slammed her hand on the neon-lit bar with determination. She confessed her intentions for the night. "Annie, I'm so horny right now. I have one goal here - to kiss a stranger. I've got to do it tonight."<br>I chuckled and patted her back. Yeonseo recently broke up with her boyfriend of 4 years. The relationship had been tumultuous, they hadn't had sex in the last year and a half. Clearly, things were pent up. <br> 
"Sure, just a kiss right? Take it easy."

Lucas brought out a small plastic bag with little white octagonal tablets. He placed one on the centre of his tongue, grinned vacantly, and swallowed. By the time we got on the dance floor, it was 1am. Red and white lasers beamed over our heads, and we were deep in the shadows of a hundred writhing bodies. The music was a blur of Pitbull, Radiohead, and Afrobeats. I felt the hair around my neck cling with sweat as the night unravelled. 

My phone was dead, I had no idea how we'd get home. Lucas' pupils had dilated like two black holes. Nisha and Olly were missing. Still nobody wanted to leave this dirty, pungent, overstimulating place. Because here, you didn't have to think. You could be awake, and not think at all.

Yeonseo was so drunk, her sense of inhibitions were 6ft under. I kept half an eye, not that she needed it. Often, she would look at a man, her focus dark and unrecognisable to me. It was a matter of the flesh, the animal inside. Within seconds, he'd be standing beside her. One such man has dreadlocks that kept falling into his face as he leaned in to talk. After they tapped something on their phones, he held her shoulder and tilted his head forward. This was the moment. Instead Yeonseo pulled away and scanned around, and I realised she was looking for me. I got her a cup of water.

Nisha and Olly were hooking up in the VIP section round the back. So Mateo, who sort of became the leader, recommended us four just share an Uber home. We rode in silence, a sense of closeness already loud in the car. After I hugged the boys goodbye, and it was just me and Yeonseo in the seats, she started crying. I patted her back gently.
